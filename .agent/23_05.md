### 2. What is TF-IDF?
**TF-IDF** stands for **Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency**. It is a metric used to represent text as numbers by calculating how important a word is to a specific tweet relative to the entire dataset:

1. **Term Frequency (TF)**: How often a word appears in a single tweet. If a word appears multiple times in a tweet, its score goes up.
2. **Inverse Document Frequency (IDF)**: How common or rare the word is across **all tweets** in your dataset. 
   * Words like `"stock"`, `"market"`, or `"the"` appear in almost every tweet, so their IDF is extremely **low** (they are treated as generic noise).
   * Words like `"bankrupt"`, `"downgrades"`, or `"surge"` appear in very few tweets, so their IDF is extremely **high** (they are treated as highly informative sentiment signals).

$$\text{TF-IDF} = \text{TF} \times \text{IDF}$$

---

### 3. What are Unigrams and Bigrams?
These are contiguous sequences of $N$ words extracted from a sentence:

* **Unigrams ($N=1$)**: Single, isolated words.
  * *Example tweet*: `"Needham downgrades Netflix"`
  * *Unigrams*: `["needham", "downgrades", "netflix"]`
  * *Limitation*: Loses all word-order context. (e.g. `"not bullish"` is treated as `"not"` and `"bullish"`, which can completely confuse a model).
* **Bigrams ($N=2$)**: Pairs of adjacent words.
  * *Bigrams*: `["needham downgrades", "downgrades netflix"]`
  * *Why they are crucial*: They capture phrase-level meaning and negation context (e.g., `"earnings beat"`, `"price target"`, `"underperform needham"`, `"not buy"`).
